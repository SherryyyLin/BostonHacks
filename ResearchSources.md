## Evidence caveats

- **BU exact primary dates:** later direct retrieval of the organizer’s Luma JSON-LD confirmed **November 14, 2026, 9 AM–10 PM ET**, a free InStock offer and public coordinates. The earlier readable prose omitted the year; the structured primary data resolves that limitation. The app follows the local one-day schedule, rather than the next UTC day shown by some aggregators.
- **WHACK and HKS access:** direct text fetching was unavailable, so dates, restrictions and schedules were verified from indexed text on the official domains. These records disclose that limitation in their app notes.
- **Unpublished hours:** Tufts’ official calendar lists an all-day November 14–15 event; MIT Policy confirms November 20–22 but not a complete schedule. Their JSON uses day boundaries solely for date filtering. The displayed schedule explicitly tells users to check exact hours.
- **Venue precision:** Tufts’ event is Anderson 206, **202 College Avenue**, rather than the entrepreneurship center’s footer address. BC’s event FAQ identifies The Hatchery at **245 Beacon Street**, rather than BC’s generic footer address. BostonHacks’ own Directions link identifies **George Sherman Union, 775 Commonwealth Avenue**.
- **HKS room:** [Harvard Growth Lab’s room listing](https://growthlab.hks.harvard.edu/event/cid-speaker-series-how-cheap-smartphones-and-off-the-shelf-machine-learning-are-changing-the-government-in-pakistan/) corroborates Perkins R429, Rubenstein fourth floor, **79 JFK Street**. MIT Policy has no confirmed building, so its address is left blank. SaaS Destroyer has no published street address, so its directions are also hidden. WHACK uses the published building name, not an invented street address.
- **Registration freshness:** BC’s FAQ gives an October 18 deadline without a year, while the current homepage confirms November 15, 2026. The app therefore says to check the organizer instead of claiming registration is open. A link to an event page never guarantees a place.
- **Edition checks:** older MLH BostonHacks pages, 2021 NNHSHacks, and past HackMIT/HackHarvard/HackBeanpot editions were not reused as upcoming 2026 dates. No 2027 recurrence date was inferred.

The geographic scope is **Greater Boston**, including Cambridge, Medford, Chestnut Hill and Wellesley, plus the explicitly online Boston listing. This is a researched collection, not proof that every local event has been found. Original organizers remain the authority for schedule changes, cancellations, eligibility and admission.


## Continuously discoverable public sources

- **Luma Boston public discovery/search:** the city’s public index and observed paginated discovery API return actual start/end timestamps, event URLs, public/hidden location visibility, ticket availability, waitlist and approval flags. Queries include hackathon, datathon and game jam, with title filtering to exclude planning and information sessions. [Boston index](https://luma.com/boston).
- **TOA public iCalendar:** [Organizer calendar](https://the-open-accelerator.com/calendar/) links to a no-key subscription. iCalendar supplies dates and location but does not establish ticket inventory.
- **MIT and Northeastern Localist:** the backend uses their observed public `/api/2/events/search` endpoints and a rolling window within the API’s 370-day maximum. Actual successful responses can contain zero qualifying activities; an info session or class referencing a hackathon is not relabeled as the hackathon. [MIT events](https://calendar.mit.edu/), [Northeastern events](https://calendar.northeastern.edu/).

The included backend was run against all seven configured source jobs on November 9, 2026; all succeeded and generated **16 deduplicated records** with source-health metadata. The collector is now deployed in [SherryyyLin/BostonHacks](https://github.com/SherryyyLin/BostonHacks); its [first successful GitHub Actions run](https://github.com/SherryyyLin/BostonHacks/actions/runs/42716474929) on November 9 at 5:58 PM Eastern repeated all seven source jobs successfully and committed the 16-record feed. Its default-branch schedule repeats every three hours, subject to GitHub queue delays. In-app foreground checks run independently; the app cannot execute indefinitely while suspended.

No fixed event count is a claim of complete coverage. New records can appear as publishers add them; disappearing listings are not automatically assumed cancelled. Hidden addresses/coordinates are not exposed as native map pins. Source status remains a link to the organizer’s current admission page.

## Boston landmarks and decorative motifs

**Make Way for Ducklings** is Nancy Schön’s bronze sculpture of Mrs. Mallard and eight ducklings in the Public Garden, near **Beacon and Charles Streets**. Both [the artist’s own record](https://www.schon.com/public/ducklings-boston.php) and [Friends of the Public Garden](https://friendsofthepublicgarden.org/our-parks/the-garden/) establish its identity and location. A map location should be resolved from that published landmark name, not the park steward’s office address.

**Freedom Trail — Boston Common starting point** uses the official visitor-center address, **139 Tremont Street, Boston, MA 02111**, published by [the Freedom Trail Foundation](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/trail-sites/boston-common) and its [directions page](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/visit/directions-parking). A start-point pin is not a surveyed rendering of the entire trail.

**Pink Chicken Project — MIT Museum:** the MIT clarification led to the real work by **Nonhuman Nonsense**, displayed in **Gene Cultures**. An [official MIT News opening article](https://news.mit.edu/2022/mit-museum-opening-0930) includes a gallery photograph whose description explicitly identifies a pink chicken. The [artists’ own project page](https://nonhuman-nonsense.com/pink-chicken-project) lists the MIT exhibition as **October 2, 2022–2024**, under previous exhibitions. A [participating artist’s university announcement](https://art.cmu.edu/news/faculty-news/rich-pell-exhibition-university-of-michigan/) also describes the exhibition’s final month in 2024. This establishes its identity and past display, **not current availability** or a permanent outdoor sculpture. The map therefore identifies MIT Museum and tells users to check current displays.

MIT Museum’s address is **314 Main Street, E28, Cambridge, MA 02142**, confirmed by the [official MIT directory](https://officesdirectory.mit.edu/mit-museum). [MIT Capital Projects](https://capitalprojects.mit.edu/projects/mit-museum) also publishes the museum building’s coordinates, **42.3623, −71.0868**; this is a museum location, not an exact indoor object position. The museum’s own exhibition page returned HTTP 429 during this check, so it was not used to claim a current display.

Other optional Freedom Trail highlights link individually to the Foundation’s records: [Massachusetts State House](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/trail-sites/massachusetts-state-house), [Faneuil Hall](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/trail-sites/faneuil-hall), and [Old North Church](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/trail-sites/old-north-church). The feature displays landmark pins, not an invented complete walking route. All sightseeing destinations remain separate from hackathon venues and visually distinct from the 🤖 event markers.

LinkedIn is **a separately reviewed discovery source, not one of the automated collector’s seven jobs**. [LinkedIn’s official Events API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/event-management/events) requires separately granted organization permissions; it does not provide unrestricted citywide event discovery. The app therefore must not claim reliable unattended LinkedIn scraping.
